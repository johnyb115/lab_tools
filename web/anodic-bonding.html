How the model works

  • Hot Pyrex conducts through Na⁺ ions. Resistivity follows Corning 7740 data (log₁₀ρ = 8.1 Ω·cm at 250 °C, 6.6 at 350 °C), so it drops about 18× between 300 °C and 400 °C.
  • The glass is a resistive sheet. Current enters at the electrode, runs sideways, and can only go down into the Si where the glass already touches.
  • Where it touches, Na⁺ moves away from the interface and leaves a space-charge layer whose voltage grows as Q²/(2εeN), with εr = 4.6 and N = 1.7×10²⁷ m⁻³ (4 wt% Na₂O). At 800 V it holds about 0.4 mC/cm²; once full, that spot carries no more current.
  • The gap closes wherever the lifted glass is more than the gap-closing voltage away from the Si. Flat, clean wafers need tens of volts; bow and particles need more.
  • No time constants are fitted. The speed follows from glass resistivity, thickness and that charge.

Compared with published bonds

  • Luo et al. bonded 4" Pyrex to Si at 300 °C and 800 V with a point electrode: about 1.5 h for the whole wafer, part still unbonded after 30 min. The model gives about 45 min with a 50 V gap-closing voltage and about 80 min with 200 V.
  • In the same work a planar electrode bonded nearly the whole area at once and reached their 30 mA current limit. The model starts at about 48 mA for a Ø85 mm plate at 300 °C.
  • Not modelled: the slow later stage (depleted layer growing to about a micrometre, bond strengthening), which is why recipes hold the voltage longer than the front needs. The glass is assumed to be at the set temperature; in vacuum it can be tens of degrees below the chuck.
